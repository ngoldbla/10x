# What it takes to win an Apple Design Award, as of 2026

Research digest, August 2026. Compiled for this audit by a research agent working
from Apple Newsroom, Apple Developer news and the ADA pages, MacStories and
Wikipedia.

**Method caveat:** `WebSearch` returned zero results throughout that session, so
this comes from direct page fetches plus DuckDuckGo HTML result pages used as a
search substitute. Independent design-press *analysis* is thin here as a result;
Apple's own ADA Q&A series is the richest source and is Apple-authored, which is
worth weighting accordingly — it reflects what Apple chose to publish about its
own picks.

---

## ADA 2026 — categories and winners

Announced **June 2, 2026**, ahead of WWDC26. 12 winners from **36 finalists**
(6 categories × 1 app + 1 game winner, plus 2 app + 2 game finalists each).
Sources: [Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-reveals-winners-of-the-2026-apple-design-awards/),
[Developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=vbvsocwh),
[the awards page](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/),
[MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/2026-apple-design-awards-winners-announced/).

**Six categories, confirmed** — no Spatial Computing (dropped after its one-year
2024 run). Apple's own descriptions:

| Category | Apple's description |
|---|---|
| Delight and Fun | "provide memorable, engaging, and satisfying experiences enhanced by Apple technologies" |
| Inclusivity | "provide a great experience for all by reflecting a variety of backgrounds, abilities, and languages" |
| Innovation | "provide a state-of-the-art experience through a novel use of Apple technologies that sets them apart in their genre" |
| Interaction | "deliver intuitive interfaces and effortless controls that are perfectly tailored to their platform" |
| Social Impact | "improve lives in a meaningful way and shine a light on crucial issues" |
| Visuals and Graphics | "feature stunning imagery, skillfully drawn interfaces, and high-quality animations with a distinctive and cohesive theme" |

### Winners

| Category | App | Game |
|---|---|---|
| Delight and Fun | **grug** (Ocho, NL) | **Is This Seat Taken?** (Poti Poti, ES) |
| Inclusivity | **Guitar Wiz** (Bijoy Thangaraj, IN) | **Pine Hearts** (Hyper Luminal, UK) |
| Innovation | **NBA: Live Games & Scores** (visionOS) | **Blue Prince** (Dogubomb, US) |
| Interaction | **Moonlitt** (Flipping Hues, IT) | **Sago Mini Jinja's Garden** (Sago Mini, CA) |
| Social Impact | **Primary: News in Depth** (visionOS) | **Consume Me** (Hsia & Thomson) |
| Visuals and Graphics | **Tide Guide** (Condor Digital) | **Cyberpunk 2077 Ultimate** (CD PROJEKT RED) |

### Apple's own rationales — the most useful material here

**grug** (Delight and Fun): *"it's the scribbled design that really pops; it's a
little masterpiece of clever simplicity that never takes itself too seriously.
(Check out the app's hand-drawn status bar, and its release notes.)…* **there's no
login, no cloud syncing, nothing extraneous** *— just a simple idea."*

**Sago Mini Jinja's Garden** (Interaction): *"a masterclass in intuitive
interaction… With interactions that require no reading and effortless
swipe-to-move controls…* **Once again, Sago Mini has put enormous effort into
perfecting something simple.**"

**Is This Seat Taken?** (Delight and Fun): *"Plenty of attention is lavished on
surrounding elements: Tapping objects in each scene produces playful and
unexpected responses, and the interface hides its own Easter eggs.* **With no
timers but lots of smiles**, *…worth keeping close at hand."*

**Primary** (Social Impact): *"And with a* **properly minimal UI**, *Primary
wisely gets out of the way of the news."*

**Moonlitt** (Interaction): *"this SwiftUI-built utility brings a* **simple
elegance**… *With broad platform support — check it out on Apple Vision Pro —*
**best-in-class Liquid Glass integration**, *and easy onboarding."*

**Guitar Wiz** (Inclusivity): *"Robust VoiceOver integration provides spoken
information and instructions on everything from pitch to chord guidance to where
players should place their fingers on the frets. Guitar Wiz also supports Dynamic
Type, Increased Contrast, and Differentiate Without Color to enable people of all
abilities to play* **with autonomy**."

**Pine Hearts** (Inclusivity): *"the game* **clearly communicates its inclusivity
options even before play begins**, *through settings that let players enhance
text legibility, customize controls, and ease both motion and sensory feedback."*

## ADA 2025, for comparison

Announced June 3, 2025; same six categories. Winners: CapWords / **Balatro**
(Delight and Fun) · Speechify / **Art of Fauna** (Inclusivity) · Play / PBJ — The
Musical (Innovation) · Taobao / DREDGE (Interaction) · Watch Duty / Neva (Social
Impact) · Feather: Draw in 3D / Infinity Nikki (Visuals).

**Two 2025 results matter enormously for a sudoku app:**

- **Balatro** (solo dev, Canada) — a deliberately lo-fi card/puzzle roguelike on
  iPhone, iPad, **Mac, Apple TV** and Vision Pro. Won Delight and Fun over vastly
  more expensive competition. Proof that a small, systems-driven, visually
  restrained game can take the top slot.
- **Art of Fauna** (Klemens Strasser, Austria) — an indie puzzle game that **won
  Inclusivity outright**. Apple's citation names puzzles solvable by
  *rearranging card images **or** re-ordering text* — two parallel input
  modalities, one inherently VoiceOver-friendly — plus colour themes, full
  VoiceOver, and **phobia settings**. This is the single most instructive
  precedent in the dataset: a tiny puzzle game beat funded studios on
  accessibility depth alone.

## What Apple's rationale language rewards

Reading 2022–2026 citations together, the recurring values:

1. **"Perfecting something simple."** Subtraction is praised explicitly, and
   named as *effort* rather than absence.
2. **Attention lavished on non-essential surfaces** — grug's hand-drawn status
   bar and release notes; Is This Seat Taken?'s tappable scene objects; Tide
   Guide's palette matching the sky through the day. Craft where nobody required
   it is almost a signature move in these citations.
3. **"Tailored to their platform"** / "best-in-class Liquid Glass integration".
   Adopting the current design language is named directly — in 2026 both the
   Interaction and Visuals app winners were cited for Liquid Glass.
4. **A world, not a theme.** "feels like its own little world", "genuinely
   lived-in", "distinctive and cohesive theme". Coherence beats richness.
5. **Autonomy** as the accessibility endpoint — "to enable people of all
   abilities to play with autonomy".
6. **Absence of pressure.** "no timers but lots of smiles"; "There's no timer, no
   pressure". Apple repeatedly praises games that remove pressure mechanics.
7. **Solo/small-team authorship is highlighted, never hidden.**

## Patterns among winners

**Restraint wins, and the winners say so.** Ausiàs Dalmau of Poti Poti on *Is
This Seat Taken?* ([ADA Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=z12xq8fa)):

> "We initially included more complex ideas, but after the first playtests, we
> realized that **limiting the design was better for the game**."

> "everything in the game exists for a reason… We designed it by asking
> ourselves, **'Is this necessary? Does this add value to the game?' If the answer
> was no, we removed it.** Sometimes in game development, it feels easier to fix
> design issues by adding more systems and making it more complex — **we tried to
> do the opposite**."

**A distinctive point of view beats polish — and the hard version of the idea is
the one that wins.** Michel Elings of Ocho on *grug*
([Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ux44ymcr)) rejected existing
handwritten fonts as "too clean and polished" and built a custom stroke-data
rendering engine:

> "Using it everywhere was much harder — it meant we had to build rendering,
> layout, animation, sizing, icons, and interaction states around our own stroke
> data. But that decision became the thing that makes grug feel like grug. **If we
> had compromised… grug would probably feel like a quote app with a cute font.
> Instead, it feels like its own little world.**"

Their advice: *"Build with the platform, not against it."*

**Spectacle wins only where spectacle is the category.** Big-budget technical
showpieces (Cyberpunk 2077 2026, Infinity Nikki 2025, Lies of P 2024, Resident
Evil Village 2023) cluster almost exclusively in **Visuals and Graphics**. Every
other 2026 category went to something small, opinionated and restrained.

**Complexity should be invisible, not absent.** Moonlitt's Nicolas Mariniello:
*"Moonlitt brings together many layers: astronomical calculations, lunar cycles…
None of that is simple, but* **the goal was never to make people feel the
complexity**."

**Appearing in two categories is a strong signal.** In 2026 Tide Guide, Sago Mini
and TR-49 each appeared twice. Depth that reads across multiple criteria is what
separates winners from finalists.

**Team size is not a barrier.** 2026: Ocho = 2 people, Poti Poti = 2, Flipping
Hues = 3, Guitar Wiz = 1. 2025: Balatro, Art of Fauna, PBJ all solo.

## tvOS and watchOS specifically

**No ADA winner has ever won *for* being a tvOS app.** tvOS appears only as one
platform among several on entries that won on other merits:

- **2026:** Sago Mini Jinja's Garden (Interaction winner), Tide Guide (Visuals
  winner), PowerWash Simulator (finalist) — 3 of 36 entries.
- **2025:** Balatro (winner), puffies., Skate City: New York.
- **2024:** Crayola Adventures (Inclusivity winner) + five finalists.
- **2023:** **stitch.** (Lykke Studios) — Inclusivity **winner**, a *puzzle game*
  on iPhone/iPad/Mac/**Apple TV** with custom options for colour blindness, low
  vision and motion sensitivity. **The closest existing analogue to Nine.**

**watchOS fares better as a winning surface** — 2026 winners Guitar Wiz, Moonlitt
and Tide Guide all ship watchOS.

**Read for Nine:** tvOS presence has never itself been the winning fact, but it
recurs on winners as evidence of the "broad platform support" Apple praised in
Moonlitt's citation. The realistic path is to win on **Interaction, Delight and
Fun, or Inclusivity *with* tvOS in the platform list** — not to win as "the great
tvOS app". And note: **no 2026 winner interview discusses tvOS-specific design
work at all.** Great tvOS interaction design is essentially unclaimed territory
in ADA history — which is either the opportunity or the reason it has never won.

## The bar for a puzzle game

Puzzle games win regularly and almost never on spectacle: Is This Seat Taken?
(2026), Art of Fauna (2025), stitch. (2023), Railbound (2023), Knotwords and
Automatoys (2023 finalists), Moncage and Letter Rooms (2022 finalists). **Sudoku's
genre is not a handicap; it is the winner's home turf.**

What Nine would need:

1. **A defensible point of view, held past the point of convenience.** The grug
   lesson: the version that is harder to build is the one that stops it feeling
   like "a sudoku app with a nice theme". A committed pixel/retro content layer
   under zero-visible-chrome is exactly that kind of stance — *provided it is
   carried through every surface*, including the unglamorous ones Apple's
   citations specifically name (status bar, release notes, empty states).
2. **Subtraction as the visible design act.** Both 2026 game winners describe
   cutting features. For sudoku the equivalents are pressure mechanics — and
   Apple praised the *absence of timers* twice in 2026. (Nine already removed the
   daily and the streak; its timer is on by default.)
3. **Interaction tailored per platform, not ported.** "Effortless controls
   perfectly tailored to their platform" is the Interaction criterion verbatim.
   A Siri Remote sudoku that feels native rather than translated is an unusually
   novel story, because almost nobody has told it.
4. **Current Apple design language.** Liquid Glass was named in two of six 2026
   app citations. Near table stakes for Interaction and Visuals.
5. **Inclusivity is the most winnable category for a small puzzle team.**

Ranked target categories: **Inclusivity** (Art of Fauna and stitch. prove it),
**Interaction** (if the tvOS control story is genuinely novel), **Delight and
Fun** (if the world-building is committed). Visuals and Graphics is dominated by
Metal/ray-tracing showpieces and is the weakest fit.

## The Inclusivity bar, specifically

- **VoiceOver that conveys *domain* information, not just labels.** Guitar Wiz
  speaks finger placement on frets, and its tuner *"can guide people by indicating
  whether a string is flat, sharp, or in tune"* — usable without looking. The
  sudoku analogue is spoken grid position, candidate state and constraint
  conflicts, not "button, cell".
- **Custom responses to system settings, beyond what the system does free.**
  Thangaraj's proudest detail: *"instead of simply relying on the system-level
  contrast change, when Increase Contrast is enabled, Guitar Wiz renders chord
  diagrams in black and white."*
- **The standard four minimum:** VoiceOver, Dynamic Type, Increase Contrast,
  Differentiate Without Color.
- **Accessibility surfaced before first play** (Pine Hearts) — *"an accessibility
  onboarding flow, to allow them to make choices before gameplay begins."*
- **Breadth: 10+ settings**, including high contrast, font scaling, simplified
  controls, control remapping.
- **From the start, not bolted on:** *"The goal was to make Guitar Wiz feel
  inclusive without making accessibility feel separate from the main design."*
  Apple praised Sago Mini for integrating inclusivity *"so organically that
  players and caregivers may not even notice."*
- **Parallel modalities** — Art of Fauna's images-or-text solving is the highest
  form: an alternate way to *play*, not merely an alternate way to read.

## What could not be found

- **No published judging process or criteria.** Neither Apple nor Wikipedia
  documents who judges or whether developers can submit. The widely-repeated
  belief that Apple's editorial and evangelism teams select internally has **no
  authoritative source** and is not asserted here.
- **No "Behind the Design" long-form articles for 2026** — that series appears
  replaced by the shorter ADA Q&A format.
- Minor date conflict: Newsroom is stamped June 2, 2026 (used here); one
  aggregator says June 3.
- MacStories' finalists piece says 18 finalists; Apple says 36. Apple's number
  counts apps *and* games across six categories and is correct.
